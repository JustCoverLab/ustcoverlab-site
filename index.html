How each bet type is defined

Every bet starts the same way: we take Pinnacle's odds (the sharpest market in the world), strip out their built-in cut to get a fair price, and compare it to the apps people actually bet on. The bet types differ in which edges they take and how much they bet. 1 unit = $50 (1% of the starting bankroll); see Units & bankroll management.

Overall

Every model bet at standard size, all in one paper bankroll that started at $5,000: straight bets, player props (counted at $50 per unit, which is 1% of the bankroll) and the ratings gap bets. This is the honest "everything" number.

Straight bets

Spreads, totals and moneylines where an app's price is at least 3% better than the fair price (4% if the bet is placed 8+ hours before the game). Sized with a quarter of the Kelly formula and never more than 3% of the bankroll on one bet. Part of Overall.

Props

Player props (points, rebounds, assists, threes, receiving yards, catches and so on) at real apps, with the same 3%+ edge rule against Pinnacle's fair price. NFL props are Unders only, because Overs lost money in testing. Graded at a flat 1 unit ($50) each so a +150 and a −150 count the same.

Aggressive

The same qualifying bets sized bigger, on a separate $5,000 paper bankroll: half Kelly (vs a quarter), up to 5% on one bet, 4% on one game and 30% of the bankroll open at once. It also takes props (quarter Kelly, max 1.5%) and teasers/parlays built only from legs with 3%+ edge. Bigger swings both ways: this is the "what if we pressed" test.

Best Bets

The highest-quality subset, on its own $5,000 paper bankroll: edge between 2% and 10% (huge edges are often bad data), odds between −250 and +250, no fresh injury news, weather flags or unknown app limits, and at most 5 per day. Same sizing rules as straight bets.

Ratings gap bets

Side tests of our own power ratings: when our line differs from the market by a set number of points (for example 5+ in college basketball), we log a paper bet. Shown in Overall and in "Everything else we've tried."

Lab Notes

Ideas we've tested against real data, whatever the answer was. Got a theory? Pitch it to @JustCoverLab on X (x.com/JustCoverLab) and we'll test it.

No edge

NBA back-to-backs and travel

4,138 games. Teams on a back-to-back are about 2 points worse, but the market already prices that in. Betting against tired teams didn't beat the line.

Edge (so far)

Only bet 3%+ edges

Bets under 3% EV lost money (−2.4%). Bets at 3% or more returned +15.1%. The model now skips anything under 3%, and needs 4% for bets placed 8+ hours early.

Edge (so far)

Bet close to game time

Bets placed under 3 hours before start beat the closing line 87% of the time, vs 46% for bets 8–24 hours out. Late news moves lines toward us when we wait.

Promising, small sample

Props when a teammate is newly out

NBA player props right after a teammate is ruled out hit 62% (fair odds said 50%), +29.6% ROI. Only 90 props from about 15 games, so it's being tracked, not trusted yet.

Cut

NFL player prop Overs

NFL Overs returned −27% while Unders returned +25%. The model now bets NFL props Unders only.

How we do it: A, B, C, D

Four habits anyone can copy. You don't need a model to use them, just a little discipline.

A

Enjoy it

Betting should make the game more fun to watch, not make you miserable. Pick games you'll actually watch, and keep "just for fun" bets on a set monthly budget so a sweat never turns into stress.

How we do it: fun bets come from a fixed $300 a month at about $20 each. No 50-to-1 parlays, no chasing. Every big game gets a pregame card with the best prices and what to watch for.
B

Give yourself a real shot

You can't control who wins. You can control the price you get. Bet when an app's price is better than the fair price, not because you have a feeling. Good bets still lose often; good prices win over time.

How we do it: we only bet when an app beats Pinnacle's fair price by 3% or more.
C

Stay in the game

Set a bankroll you could lose entirely without it mattering, and keep it separate. Make 1 unit about 1% of it. Small, steady bets survive the cold streaks that wipe out big ones, and every bettor hits cold streaks.

How we do it: 1 unit = $50 on a $5,000 paper bankroll, a quarter of the Kelly formula, and never more than 3% on one bet.
D

Track what matters

If you don't track it, you don't know. And if you don't know, you've already lost. Log every bet: the price, the stake, the result, and whether you beat the closing line. Your memory keeps the wins; the record keeps the truth. Money is hard to earn. Don't give it away.

How we do it: every bet is timestamped before the game and stays on this site, win or lose.

How the model works

  1. Start from the sharpest price. Pinnacle's odds are the best estimate of a game's true chances. We remove their built-in cut to get a fair price.
  2. Shop every app. We compare that fair price to the odds at the apps regular people use.
  3. Only bet real edges. A bet qualifies when an app's price is at least 3% better than fair.
  4. Size bets small. Stakes are a quarter of the Kelly formula and never more than 3% of the bankroll on one bet, so no single game can sink us. Details below.
  5. Grade the price, not just the result. The real scorecard is closing-line value: did we get a better number than where the line closed? That says whether we're right long before win-loss does.
  6. Test everything in public. New ideas get backtested, and the results get posted in Lab Notes, even when the idea fails.

Units & bankroll management

How much we bet matters as much as what we bet. This is the part most bettors skip.

1 unit = $50

One unit is 1% of the $5,000 starting bankroll, and it stays fixed at $50 even as the bankroll grows or shrinks. That way "+10u" means the same thing in week 1 and week 20. Props are always exactly 1 unit. Straight bets average about 0.7u, because most edges are small.

Bet size follows the edge

We size straight bets with the Kelly formula, which turns an edge and the odds into a bet size: bigger edge, bigger bet. Full Kelly swings too hard, and our edge estimates are never perfect, so we bet a quarter of what it says. Example: a 4% edge at −110 works out to about 1.1u ($55).

Hard caps

Never more than 3u (3%) on one straight bet. The Aggressive bankroll goes up to half Kelly, with at most 5% on one bet, 4% on one game and 30% of the bankroll riding at once. Those caps are the point of that test: how much extra swing do bigger bets buy?

Why so small?

Even a real edge loses a lot. A good bet wins maybe 53–56% of the time, so losing streaks of 6 to 8 bets happen to everyone. At about 1% a bet, a bad week costs a few percent of the bankroll, not the bankroll.

Separate bankrolls

Overall, Aggressive and Best Bets each run on their own $5,000 paper bankroll, so a hot streak in one can't hide a cold streak in another.

If you bet your own money

Pick a bankroll you could lose entirely without it mattering, and keep it separate from everything else. Make 1 unit 1–2% of it. Don't raise your unit after a hot week, don't chase after a bad one, and resize at most once a month. A bankroll plan is what makes betting fun for the long haul instead of stressful.
